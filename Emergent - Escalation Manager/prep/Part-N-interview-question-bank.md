## Section 1 — Basic (Q1–Q22)

Fundamentals. You must answer these instantly and without hesitation.

| # | Question | Answer in brief | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is an escalation? | A case needing elevated ownership, urgency, or authority because it outgrew the normal process. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 2 | Escalation vs incident? | Incident = the event (service interrupted). Escalation = the handling (elevated ownership). Independent of each other. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 3 | What triggers an escalation? | Impact, urgency, process failure, relationship breakdown, or risk. Any one suffices. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 4 | SLA vs SLO vs SLI? | SLI = measured value. SLO = internal target. SLA = contractual promise with a remedy. SLO stricter than SLA. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 5 | What is CSAT? | Satisfaction with a single interaction, usually 1–5, reported as % positive. Transactional. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 6 | What is NPS? | "Would you recommend us?" 0–10; promoters minus detractors. Relationship-level, slow-moving. | [K](Part-K-metrics-and-trend-analysis.md) |
| 7 | Support vs Customer Success? | Support is reactive (customer raises an issue). Success is proactive (drives value and retention). | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 8 | What are support tiers? | L1 common issues, L2 deeper troubleshooting, L3 specialists, then Engineering. Ownership transfers upward. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 9 | What is swarming? | Pulling specialists to a case while one owner retains it — no handoffs, no lost context. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 10 | What is SaaS? | Software rented as a service running on the vendor's infrastructure, rather than installed and owned. | [B](Part-B-saas-cloud-ai-fundamentals.md) |
| 11 | What is multi-tenancy? | Many customers sharing infrastructure, separated in software. Like an apartment block with shared plumbing. | [B](Part-B-saas-cloud-ai-fundamentals.md) |
| 12 | Outage vs degradation? | Outage = it doesn't work. Degradation = it works badly (slow or partly failing). | [B](Part-B-saas-cloud-ai-fundamentals.md) |
| 13 | What is a regression? | Something that previously worked is now broken, usually after a change. | [B](Part-B-saas-cloud-ai-fundamentals.md) |
| 14 | What is a rollback? | Reverting to the previous known-good version. Usually the fastest mitigation. | [B](Part-B-saas-cloud-ai-fundamentals.md) |
| 15 | What is an LLM? | A model trained on text that predicts the next chunk — sophisticated autocomplete. Fluency ≠ accuracy. | [B](Part-B-saas-cloud-ai-fundamentals.md) |
| 16 | What is a hallucination? | Confident, plausible, false output generated by a model. | [B](Part-B-saas-cloud-ai-fundamentals.md) |
| 17 | What is RCA? | Root Cause Analysis — structured investigation into why something happened, to prevent recurrence. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 18 | What is MTTR? | Mean Time To Restore/Resolve — but the "R" is ambiguous; always clarify which. | [K](Part-K-metrics-and-trend-analysis.md) |
| 19 | Severity vs priority? | Severity = how bad (objective). Priority = what we do first (business decision). | [C](Part-C-escalation-lifecycle.md) |
| 20 | What is a status page? | A public page showing service health — the highest-leverage incident communication tool. | [D](Part-D-incident-management.md) |
| 21 | What is a postmortem? | Written post-incident analysis covering impact, timeline, causes, and actions. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 22 | What is churn? | A customer cancelling. The outcome most escalation decisions are ultimately weighed against. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |

| 39 | What is BLUF? | Bottom Line Up Front — conclusion first, evidence after. How executives read. | [G](Part-G-communication-under-pressure.md) |
| 40 | Why is "sorry for any inconvenience" bad? | Generic, conditional, apologizes for their reaction rather than the actual damage. | [G](Part-G-communication-under-pressure.md) |
| 41 | What is a service credit? | A pre-agreed contractual remedy for missing an SLA; usually the customer's sole remedy. | [H](Part-H-billing-refunds-compensation.md) |
| 42 | Refund vs credit? | Refund returns money (clean exit). Credit offsets future invoices (keeps the relationship). | [H](Part-H-billing-refunds-compensation.md) |
| 43 | Controller vs processor? | Controller decides why/how data is processed; processor acts on instruction. Usually customer = controller. | [I](Part-I-financial-legal-reputational-risk.md) |
| 44 | What is prompt injection? | Malicious instructions hidden in content the model reads, hijacking behavior. A **security** issue. | [B](Part-B-saas-cloud-ai-fundamentals.md) |

## Section 3 — Advanced (Q45–Q106)

Judgement, trade-offs, and scenarios. These carry the interview.

### Escalation judgement

| # | Question | Answer in brief | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 | Walk me through an escalation end to end. | Intake → qualify → triage → single owner + cadence → parallel technical/emotional tracks → mitigate → resolve → RCA → verified close with customer agreement. | [C](Part-C-escalation-lifecycle.md) |
| 46 | Product works as designed but the customer is furious. Escalation? | Usually yes — escalation is about handling, not defect existence. Fix via expectation reset, workaround, or a product-gap conversation. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 47 | Where does your ownership stop? | I own outcome, pace, comms, written record, and the systemic fix. Not the code fix, roadmap, legal position, or concessions beyond policy. | [A](Part-A-support-escalation-landscape.md) |
| 48 | How do you avoid being a bottleneck as single contact? | Single *voice* means consistent message, not sole channel. Publish the position, delegate technical dialogue, use templates. | [C](Part-C-escalation-lifecycle.md) |
| 49 | Two customers, one Sev 1 each, one of you. What do you do? | Triage on blast radius, contractual exposure, and reversibility; get a second owner assigned; be transparent about cadence rather than quietly under-serving one. | [C](Part-C-escalation-lifecycle.md) |
| 50 | A customer threatens to cancel. Does that change severity? | It changes *priority* and commercial risk, not severity. Severity stays evidence-based, or the scale becomes meaningless. | [C](Part-C-escalation-lifecycle.md) |
| 51 | What are the four emotional drivers of escalation anger? | Not being believed, informed, respected, or safe. The fourth — their internal credibility — drives executive escalations. | [C](Part-C-escalation-lifecycle.md) |
| 52 | Customer demands a named engineer on a call. | Usually decline, and explain why: the engineer is most valuable investigating. Offer a technical briefing at a defined time instead. Protect focus. | [F](Part-F-cross-functional-coordination.md) |
| 53 | When would you *reject* an escalation? | When it doesn't meet criteria — but never silently. Return it with a reason, a named owner, and a route back. | [C](Part-C-escalation-lifecycle.md) |
| 54 | How do you handle a serial escalator? | Look for the underlying pattern — often unmet expectations, a real product gap, or an unresolved earlier failure. Address the cause, and agree a working model with them. | [K](Part-K-metrics-and-trend-analysis.md) |

### Incident and RCA

| # | Question | Answer in brief | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| 55 | Restore service or find the cause first? | Restore. Capture evidence, then mitigate. Exception: when blast radius is unknown and mitigation might worsen it (suspected data corruption or security). | [D](Part-D-incident-management.md) |
| 56 | 40 minutes into a Sev 1 with no cause — what do you tell customers? | Observed impact, what's unaffected, that cause is unconfirmed, current actions, workaround, next update time. Never speculate on cause. | [D](Part-D-incident-management.md) |
| 57 | Should you post publicly for a partial outage? | Yes, scoped accurately. Affected customers already know; silence only hides your awareness and floods support. | [D](Part-D-incident-management.md) |
| 58 | What is alert fatigue and why do you care? | Noise causes non-response → late detection → larger impact. If a page isn't actionable, it shouldn't page. | [D](Part-D-incident-management.md) |
| 59 | Engineer says root cause was human error. | That's the start, not the end. Ask why the system let the mistake reach customers — Swiss cheese: which defence layers had holes? | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 60 | Same incident recurs six weeks after a great RCA. | The action graveyard: unowned or undated actions, no tracking surviving closure, or "done" without verification. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 61 | How deep should 5 Whys go? | To the deepest cause you have authority and ability to change. Above = philosophy, below = symptom. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 62 | Explain IS/IS-NOT. | Compare what is affected against what isn't across what/where/when/extent. The boundary reveals the cause. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 63 | Why does AND vs OR matter in fault trees? | AND: break one branch and prevent the whole failure — cheapest fix. OR: you must address every branch. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 64 | What's the most valuable postmortem section? | "Where we got lucky" — near-misses reveal untested defences and should generate actions. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 65 | How do you verify an action worked? | Evidence in production: alert tested by simulating failure, error rate zero across two peak cycles, runbook used successfully in a drill. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 66 | One-off or systemic — how do you tell? | Systemic: multiple independent reports, recurring tag, same failure shape from different triggers, correlation with releases or components. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |
| 67 | Incident vs problem management? | Incident restores service (speed). Problem eliminates recurrence (permanence). Different clocks and owners. | [E](Part-E-root-cause-analysis.md) |

## Section 5 — Closing and "why"

| # | Question | Direction of a strong answer |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Why do you want this role? | Connect to the specific mandate: owning critical escalations *and* fixing the systemic causes — not just firefighting. |
| C2 | Why this company? | The product category and its hard problems (correctness, reliability, security, scale) — and why escalation management matters more, not less, in autonomous products. |
| C3 | Why are you leaving your current situation? | Forward-looking, never disparaging. Scope, ownership, and impact you want next. |
| C4 | What's your biggest weakness? | A real one, with the concrete mechanism you use to manage it. |
| C5 | How do you handle stress? | Process and structure — cadence, prioritization, written records — not stoicism. |
| C6 | Where do you see yourself in three years? | Program ownership, scale, and prevention — consistent with the role's trajectory. |
| C7 | What would you do in your first 90 days? | Listen and baseline → standardize → systematize. See [Part L](Part-L-playbooks-slas-scaling.md). |
| C8 | What questions do you have for us? | Always have five. See [Part O](Part-O-behavioral-and-closing.md). |
| C9 | Why should we hire you? | Ownership under pressure, structured RCA, cross-functional delivery, and turning patterns into permanent fixes. |
| C10 | What's the hardest part of this job? | Holding customer advocacy and business protection simultaneously without collapsing into either. |
