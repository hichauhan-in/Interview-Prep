# Service Account Executive (Airline / Travel Technology) — Study Guide

> **What this is:** a complete, beginner-to-advanced study and interview-preparation guide for a **Service Account Executive (SAE)** role in airline/travel technology.
>
> **Who it's for:** anyone — no prior service-management, ITIL, or airline-industry knowledge is assumed. Every term is explained in plain English before it is used.
>
> **How it's written:** generic and role-focused. No personal or CV-specific content. Examples are illustrative, not drawn from any individual's history.

---

## How to use this guide

1. **Read in order.** Parts are sequenced so each builds on the last: foundations → core process → applied practice → interview closing.
2. **Don't skip Part A.** It defines the vocabulary (SLA, P1, MIM, PSS, GDS…) that every later Part assumes.
3. **Answer out loud.** Each Part ends with likely interview questions and model answers. Reading is not the same as being able to say it.
4. **Use the memory hooks.** Each Part closes with 30-second recall cues for last-minute revision.
5. **Finish with Parts K–M.** The question bank and behavioral prep are where knowledge converts into interview performance.
6. **Keep [Appendix A — Glossary](prep/Appendix-A-glossary.md) open beside you.** If a term is unfamiliar, look it up there rather than guessing from context.
7. **After Part F, read [Appendix B — Worked Scenario](prep/Appendix-B-worked-scenario.md).** It follows one incident end to end and shows how every technique connects — this is what makes the separate Parts click into place.

**Suggested pacing:** Parts A–B (fundamentals) → C–F (the operational core, the heart of this role) → [Appendix B](prep/Appendix-B-worked-scenario.md) to see it all connected → G–I (measurement, governance, improvement) → J (technical literacy) → K–M (edge, practice, delivery) → [Appendix C](prep/Appendix-C-quick-reference.md) as your final revision sheet.

## The role in one paragraph

A **Service Account Executive** is the customer's operational partner and single point of contact for everything service-related. When something breaks, they coordinate the response and keep the customer informed. When nothing is broken, they watch trends, run service review meetings, chase improvements, and build trust. They do not sell, and they do not personally fix the technology — they **own the outcome and orchestrate the people who do**. Think of them as the air traffic controller of the customer relationship: not flying the plane, but making sure every aircraft lands safely, in the right order, with everyone informed.

## Grouped index

### Group 1 — Foundations (start here)
1. What a Service Account Executive actually does; how the role differs from support, sales, project management, and account management
2. The airline & travel technology landscape: GDS, PSS, reservations, departure control, distribution
3. Industry vocabulary: PNR, IATA/ICAO codes, NDC, IROPS, peak season, check-in windows
4. Why airline systems are unusually unforgiving: real-time, 24/7, safety-adjacent, high-visibility
5. Service management fundamentals: what a "service" is, service value, ITIL 4 in plain English
6. The ITSM practice map: incident, problem, change, request, event, knowledge, configuration
7. Roles, responsibility models and the operational environment: RACI, support tiers, 24/7 coverage models (follow-the-sun, shift rota, on-call), and ITSM tooling

### Group 2 — The operational core (the heart of the role)
8. Incident management end-to-end: detection → logging → triage → diagnosis → resolution → closure
9. Priority vs severity vs impact vs urgency — and the priority matrix
10. Ticket ownership, hygiene, and why "updates" are a deliverable, not an afterthought
11. Major incident management (MIM): triggers, war rooms, bridge calls, roles on the bridge
12. Incident communications: cadence, audience, tone, holding statements, executive updates
13. Escalation management: functional vs hierarchical escalation, escalation paths, when to escalate
14. Handling an angry customer: de-escalation, expectation setting, saying "no" and "I don't know"
15. Post-incident review (PIR): blameless culture, timelines, action tracking
16. Problem management: reactive vs proactive, known error, workaround vs fix
17. Root cause analysis techniques: 5 Whys, Ishikawa/fishbone, fault tree, Pareto, Kepner-Tregoe
18. Change management and its link to incidents: standard/normal/emergency change, CAB, freeze periods

### Group 3 — Measurement, governance & improvement
19. SLA, SLO, OLA, underpinning contracts, service credits — and how they differ
20. Availability math: the "nines", downtime budgets, measurement windows, exclusions
21. The metric family: MTTD, MTTA, MTTR, MTBF, first-time-fix, backlog age, reopen rate
22. Customer experience metrics: CSAT, DSAT, NPS, CES — strengths and traps of each
23. Trend analysis: spotting patterns, seasonality, repeat offenders, leading vs lagging indicators
24. Service reporting: building a report a customer actually reads; storytelling with data
25. Service review meetings and governance cadence: operational review, MBR, QBR, executive review
26. Voice of the customer: capturing, quantifying, and routing feedback internally
27. Continual improvement: CSI register, PDCA, Kaizen, Lean and Six Sigma basics for service
28. Service transition: onboarding, knowledge transfer, early life support, exit criteria

### Group 4 — Technical literacy (enough to be credible, not to code)
29. How a modern service is built: clients, APIs, services, databases, queues, integrations
30. Cloud, hosting, and resilience: regions, redundancy, failover, disaster recovery, RTO/RPO
31. Networking basics that explain outages: DNS, latency, timeouts, load balancers, certificates
32. Reading the evidence: logs, traces, dashboards, alerts, correlation vs causation
33. Monitoring & observability: synthetic checks, real-user monitoring, alert fatigue
34. Security, privacy & compliance in travel: personal data, payment data, incident notification duties
35. Capacity, performance & peak readiness: load testing, seasonal surges, event-driven spikes

### Group 5 — Edge, practice & delivery
36. Miscellaneous & deeper topics: competitive landscape, NDC and modern retailing, SRE vs ITIL, AI in service operations, current industry trends
37. Interview question bank: 100+ questions with answers, graded basic → advanced, plus behavioral and closing
38. Behavioral & closing: STAR method, competency translation, ready-to-adapt stories, "why this role", questions to ask, night-before cheat sheet

## Starter glossary (full explanations arrive in the Parts)

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|------|----------------------|
| **Incident** | Something is broken or degraded right now; the goal is to restore service fast. |
| **Problem** | The underlying cause behind one or more incidents; the goal is to stop recurrence. |
| **Major incident** | A high-impact incident needing coordinated, urgent, cross-team response. |
| **Escalation** | Deliberately raising attention or authority to unblock progress. |
| **SLA** | A written promise about service performance, with consequences if missed. |
| **ITIL** | The most widely used set of best practices for managing IT services. |
| **GDS** | A global booking network that connects airlines with travel sellers. |
| **PSS** | The airline's core system for reservations, ticketing, and getting passengers onto flights. |
| **PNR** | The booking record for a passenger's trip — the "file" holding the itinerary. |
| **IROPS** | Irregular operations — delays, cancellations, disruption and the recovery from them. |
| **RCA** | Root cause analysis — the structured hunt for the real underlying cause. |
| **MTTR** | Mean time to restore/resolve — the average time to get service back. |
| **CSAT** | A direct customer satisfaction score, usually collected by survey. |
